THROUGHLINE — MARKETING STRATEGY BRIEF
Career clarity, built from what you already have.
A discussion starter for product design and branding partners.


1. THE PRODUCT IN ONE LINE

A structured self-discovery instrument that finds the single career direction
sitting at the intersection of the most interests, skills, and resources a
person already has — turning a scattered list into a unifying professional
purpose.

The mechanism: the user inventories everything they care about and can do,
rates the synergy between every pair, and the tool clusters the result to
reveal which directions hold the most of their life together. Two hours of
focused effort, one defensible answer to "what should I actually do?"


2. THE CORE INSIGHT (THE WHITE SPACE)

Existing tools assess the individual in isolation — strengths, personality,
energy patterns. None of them address the synergy BETWEEN a person's many
interests. Ikigai gestures at "purpose lives at an intersection" but offers
no method to find it. Throughline occupies the unoccupied cell: a rigorous
instrument plus the intersection thesis. We own the dimension the incumbents
ignore.

Strategic posture: we market like Pigment, not like BetterUp. As a new
entrant we name the dimension the giants overlook and plant our flag there,
rather than competing on coaching scale.


3. TARGET MARKET

Primary: the multi-passionate professional in transition. Mid-career, roughly
28-45, capable across several domains, currently fragmented or recently
displaced. They have outgrown generic personality quizzes and want analytical
rigor, not personality astrology. They already keep lists and respect a
framework. Their pain is not lack of options — it is too many, with no way to
reconcile them.

Secondary: career coaches and outplacement firms (the tool as an instrument
they run clients through), bootcamp and graduate career services, and the
portfolio-career / solopreneur crowd deciding what to build.

Disqualifier: the person who already knows what they want. This is for the
overwhelmed-by-options, not the directionless-and-passive.


4. BRAND POSITIONING

We live in the gap between two poles: the soft affirming personality-quiz
space (too fluffy, no rigor) and dry productivity tooling (too cold, no
meaning). Our register is ANALYTICAL WARMTH — rigorous method in service of a
deeply personal payoff.

Tonal anchors: clarifying, structured, earned. Not "find your bliss" but
"find your through-line."

North star: CliftonStrengths' credibility wrapped in Ikigai's intuitive
promise. We borrow the trusted-method / objective-result framing from the
incumbents and the purpose-at-an-intersection mental model from Ikigai, then
own the procedure that neither provides.

The matrix itself is the central brand motif — a grid that resolves from
noise into clusters is a literal picture of the value proposition.


5. THE LEAD CAMPAIGN CONCEPT — "MANY SELVES, ONE DIRECTION"

The hero creative dramatizes the mechanism. The arc of the spot mirrors the
arc of the tool: fragmentation resolving into focus.

OPEN (the many selves):
  We all contain many selves. The father. The musician. The programmer. The
  frisbee player on Sunday mornings.
  [Fast-scrolling, overlapping images of one person across these varied
  contexts — warm, real, slightly cluttered. The visual feeling is abundance
  bordering on overwhelm.]

TURN (the problem):
  How do we choose just one career when the world is so cluttered with
  opportunities and directions?
  [The images pile up and compete; the frame feels crowded.]

RESOLVE (the method):
  Use a structured process to find the career strategy that unites your
  greatest interests with your strongest path.
  [Cut to a person at a whiteboard — a grid, sticky notes, clusters forming.
  The clutter resolves into a single legible shape. Calm replaces noise.]

CLOSE:
  Throughline. Career clarity, built from what you already have.

Why it works: the "many selves resolving into one" structure IS the product
demo. The clutter-to-clarity visual transition is the value proposition made
literal, and it differentiates instantly from quiz-style competitors who can
only show a results screen.


6. MARKETING STRATEGY

a) DEMONSTRATION OVER DESCRIPTION
The product is hard to explain in a tagline, so lead with worked examples.
The "I like marketing AND cooking — so I could build a business marketing
food I make" style of revealed synergy is the best teaching artifact we have.
Build a library of these short, concrete before/after vignettes; each one
teaches the mechanism faster than any feature list.

b) CONTENT-LED ACQUISITION ON A NAMED PAIN
Build essays and short video around "having too many interests is a real
problem" — a pain the multi-passionate feels acutely and that existing career
content underserves. This is the Pigment register: name the gap the incumbents
ignore, in a confident analytical voice.

c) FREE LITE VERSION AS TOP OF FUNNEL
A small, fast matrix that delivers a genuine aha-moment with low friction,
gating the full deep process behind sign-up or payment. The insight has to be
reachable before the commitment is asked for.

d) COACH AND INSTITUTION PARTNERSHIPS
Career coaches and outplacement firms are both a distribution channel and a
credibility source. The tool makes their sessions more productive; they bring
clients and lend authority. Pursue the same with bootcamp and graduate career
services.

e) SEED IN EXISTING-IDENTITY COMMUNITIES
Multi-passionate, polymath, portfolio-career, and solopreneur communities
already have language for this identity. Meet them there rather than building
awareness from zero.


7. THE CENTRAL DESIGN-AND-MARKETING RISK

The pairwise-rating phase is both the source of all value and the most likely
point of abandonment — potentially hundreds of intersections to score. Every
branding and UX decision must make a tedious combinatorial chore feel like
progressive discovery rather than data entry. Candidate approaches to put on
the table: surfacing micro-insights mid-rating, intelligent ordering so
high-signal pairs come first, progress mechanics, and a "good enough"
sampling mode. This is the priority problem for the firm to solve, because the
marketing promise of "two hours, one answer" lives or dies on it.


8. COMPETITIVE REFERENCE MAP

Closest analogues (study model and funnel):
  CliftonStrengths / Gallup — the credibility and language-ownership playbook.
  16Personalities, Enneagram — the free-result-to-paid-depth funnel.
  Pigment.is — the David-vs-Goliath positioning to emulate.

Conceptual rivals (compete for the mental space; our opening):
  Ikigai — owns the intersection idea, lacks a method. Our scaffold.
  Designing Your Life (Burnett & Evans) — our intellectual lineage and tone.

Adjacent / channel (partners more than rivals):
  BetterUp, The Muse, Ama La Vida — productized coaching; tone reference.
  Creator-brands (Sho Dewan, Anna Papalia, Ken Coleman) — the content-funnel
  playbook and proof the audience and channel exist.


9. QUESTIONS FOR THE FIRM

- How do we make the rating phase feel like discovery, not data entry?
- Does the brand architecture stay single (Throughline does everything) at
  launch, splitting out a service tier only after the coach channel proves out?
- How far do we lean on the matrix-grid as visual identity vs. the
  many-selves human imagery?
- What is the single aha-moment the free lite version must deliver?
